Success story · Real estate · Philippines

A 9-step manual payables process, read and matched by an agent.

A premier Philippine real estate conglomerate ran accounts payable by hand: billing countered at a desk, encoded into a weekly spreadsheet, checked, entered into SAP, and routed for approval on paper. Volumes were on course to quadruple. The reading was the bottleneck, so that is what we pointed the agent at.

The client

A premier Philippine real estate conglomerate.

High-rise luxury residential developer, 35+ years of operations, managing properties across Metro Manila and expanding nationwide. Unnamed here under a mutual NDA.

The burning platform

Volume was the reason, not cost.

Transactions were on course to quadruple in two years. At 20 minutes of reading each, the pile grows faster than the team can be hired against — which is what makes reconciliation the right place to put an agent, and the wrong place to put more people.

The process

Nine steps became seven, and three of them are still a person.

The steps that disappeared are the ones where somebody retyped what a document already said. The review step was kept on purpose: exceptions are handled by the team, not by the agent.

How it was delivered

A two-week proof first, because the three-way match had to be shown.

Nobody had to take the extraction accuracy on trust. The first two weeks tested it on their own documents against a stated floor, and production only started after that number was met.

Phase 0 · 2 weeksProof of conceptThree-way match feasibility on real documents, 80% extraction accuracy target, three document triads validated, stakeholder sign-off. June–July 2025.
Phase 1Workflow engineDual-entity disbursement platform, role-based access for vendor, materials and AP, RFP and voucher generation, AWS deployment behind a secure portal. September 2025 onward.
Phase 2–3Visibility and scaleManagement KPI dashboards by department, admin and manager hierarchy, load tested at 6,000 transactions a month and 50+ users. Ongoing 2025–2026.
What was hard

The honest version, including what the agent did not fix.

Published because the difficulties are the useful part of a case study. Every one of these changed how the product works.

Challenges encountered
  • !Vendors submitted combined PDFs, which had to be unbundled before a three-way match could run at all.
  • !Transaction volumes more than doubled mid-engagement, raising the scalability bar after the design was set.
  • !Dual-entity support required isolated workflows on shared infrastructure.
  • !SAP integration was deferred, so the Excel-based RCDT monitoring had to be bridged as an interim.
  • !Document formats varied across 58+ vendor types, which broadened what the extraction had to handle.
What we learned
  • ›A proof of concept first de-risked the production investment and built confidence before budget was committed.
  • ›AI reduced effort per transaction, not operational complexity. Headcount was redeployed, not eliminated, and saying otherwise would have been a false promise.
  • ›Human review at the exception stage is not a transitional measure. It is what makes the throughput safe.
  • ›Phased delivery let scope be refined against feedback instead of guessed at up front.
  • ›Separating vendor, materials and AP access cut error propagation significantly.
