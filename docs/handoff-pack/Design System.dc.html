the system

One kernel and fourteen primitives. Every state of every primitive is drawn and labelled below, most of them live. The kernel is the only place a colour, a type size, a spacing unit, a duration or a layer is declared; this page declares none of the five.

This is not the product. It is the vocabulary the product is drawn in. Nothing here is deployed and every figure is illustrative. Where a primitive draws something a decision refused or left unbuilt, it is hatched, it carries the word, and it names what refused it.

Typography

Two faces, six steps, six weights, four tracking values, three leadings and three text measures. Every one of them has exactly one job, and the job is what you pick by — not the look.

The two faces, and no third

The three small steps do not scale with the viewport, and that is the rule rather than an omission. A pure vw value ignores the reader's font-size setting entirely, which fails 1.4.4 Resize Text while looking responsive.

Leading: the smallest type gets the loosest leading

That is the reverse of the instinct, and it is the whole rule. ⚠️ All three are unitless multipliers, so they multiply the element's own size — and so a reader's 1.5× override under 1.4.12 Text Spacing multiplies rather than replaces them. The six indicators

Six, each a distinct form, so none can be mistaken for another. Two are marks and four are grammar. The count is load-bearing: a seventh indicator is a design change, not an addition.

Mark one The meter — one line
0.72

Only where a real score exists, and the tick is the floor it had to clear. Never for progress, never for completion, never for anything unmeasured. A progress bar is a second line and there is not one anywhere in the system.

Mark two The connection light — one dot
ERP reachable Bank feed unreachable

Ink, never the agent green: this is infrastructure. A live connection breathes; an unreachable one is hollow and still — absence of life is the signal, so no hazard colour exists and none is needed. The words say the same thing, because a state may never depend on a light alone. The only radius in the system besides the mark.

Grammar one Tilde hedges a forecast
~4 hours to clear — an estimate, and it says so

A figure the system is guessing at wears the hedge. Nothing else does, so a number without one is a number somebody can rely on.

Grammar two Past tense states a fact
Run completed 03:12 — it happened, and here is when

The tense carries the certainty. "Completed at 03:12" is a fact with a time on it; "completing" would be a claim about now that the page cannot make.

Grammar three Chevron means your move
Needs authority › — a person is the exception, so a person gets named

The agent ran every row, so naming it says nothing. A person in the loop is the exception, and the chevron is where the work stops until one acts. Ink, not agent green.

Grammar four Ellipsis means elsewhere
Waiting on the bank feed … — blocked on something outside this system

Not "loading". Elsewhere — the work is real, it is moving, and it is not moving here. A reader who sees this knows there is nothing for them to do yet.

And two treatments that are not indicators, so the six stay six

Neither reports a state of the work, which is what keeps them out of the count. The hatch alpha is .18, not .055. At .055 the stripe composited to 1.11:1 on every ground — fainter than --rule, the faintest line the system deliberately declares, so the hatch sat below the visible floor on the ground it was calibrated for. At .18 it is 1.42:1 on both light grounds: visible in a screenshot, still unmistakably a hatch rather than a fill. The alpha form is what makes it ground-independent — an alpha composite scales the ground rather than displacing it.

Microcopy

The product's sentences are part of the system, not decoration on top of it. Two rules carry most of it: the reader is addressed when something is theirs to do, and the product never says a thing it cannot evidence.

Second person for consequences, neutral for facts

The close read neutrally and the partner portal read as you, and nobody had decided that. The rule now: a fact about the world is stated neutrally; a consequence for the reader is addressed to them. It is the difference between reporting and instructing, and mixing them is what makes software sound either cold or pushy.

The same sentence, on the right side of the line and the wrong one.

British spelling, in copy and in code

🛑 Decided, and it is not a preference. The records already carry the cost of leaving it unruled: one model holds costCenter and another holds costCentres, and the join between them is an authorisation decision — so a spelling divergence is a scope dimension that silently matches nothing. A field name that diverges from the copy is a finding, on the same standing as a hard-coded colour.

A failure is a sentence, then a code

Every failure path emits a code from a closed vocabulary, because under a restrictive telemetry profile the class of the error is the only diagnostic channel that survives. So the reader gets the sentence and the code gets the last word: a support call starts with the code, and nobody should have to describe a screen to find it.

Five things this product never writes

Each with the reason, because a word list without one gets argued with at the call site.
BannedWhy
Hedges — simply, just, easily, quicklyThey tell the reader the thing they are struggling with is easy. Every one of them is deletable with no loss, which is the test.
Cheer — Great!, All set!, You're all caught upThis is a finance close. Congratulating someone on a clean night reads as a product that does not know what is at stake — and a clean night is reported, with its denominator.
Vagueness — something went wrong, an error occurredIt is the sentence that makes a code necessary and then withholds it. If the cause genuinely cannot be determined, say which two causes look alike.
Anthropomorphism — the agent thinks, believes, wants, feelsIt claims a state of mind in place of evidence. The agent read, matched, cited and priced; those are checkable and a belief is not.
Emoji, in product copyThe system has six glyphs and no icon set, for the same reason: a second semantic channel competing with the grammar. Emoji in this record are editorial marks on a design document, not product copy.

When the product apologises

Not banned, and narrow: only where the fault is ours and it cost the reader something. A posting that failed after they left, a period that has to be re-run, a delivery that went to the wrong place. Not for a refusal — a refusal is the control working — and not for a source the customer owns going quiet. An apology everywhere is an apology nowhere, and the places above are exactly where one is owed.

The agent speaks in the first person, about acts and confidence only

🛑 The line, because "I think this is a duplicate" sits exactly on it. I is available for what the agent did and for how sure it is. It is never available for a state of mind.

Two consequences worth stating. A confidence figure always carries what it is measured against — a floor, a denominator, a threshold — because a bare percentage is a number nobody can act on. And every billable sentence carries the mark: ∴ trailing the spend, in the agent's own ink, so a person reading a cost never has to ask whether it was charged.

Figures, dates and the small conventions

  • RuleCurrency is a prefix in a quieter step — SGD then the figure — because the unit is constant down a column and the number is not.
  • RuleEvery figure that is compared is tabular. Proportional figures in a column are a rendering accident that reads as a data error.
  • RuleDates as 06 Mar, times with their zone. A close crosses three time zones and a bare clock time is a question.
  • RuleA button names the act, and the confirmation repeats it. Post it then Posted. — never Submit then Success.
  • RuleA refusal states what, why, and whose decision it was. Three parts; the third is the one people leave out and the one that stops an argument.
  • RuleNo metadiscourse. Not here's why this matters — say the thing that matters.

Motion

One spring, four product animations, and one brand animation. Everything that moves runs the spring; everything that only changes opacity or colour runs the fade. A fifth product animation is a finding, not an addition — the brand one is the mascot's, scoped to a single selector and counted apart because it reports nothing.

The spring, and what it replaced

Why linear() and not a JS spring. This is a real damped spring — ζ 0.6, ωn 18 rad/s — sampled at eleven points over 420ms and written as an easing function. It runs on the compositor as an ordinary CSS transition, needs no runtime, survives the reduced-motion guard, and cannot disagree with itself between two components the way two spring configs in two files would. What it gives up is interruptibility: a JS spring retargets mid-flight from its current velocity, and this one restarts. That trade is the reason it is one token and not a library, and it is recorded as an open finding rather than as a win.

The four product animations, and nothing else

Everything else that moves is a transition on a property, timed by --t-spring or --t-fade. Amended 3 Oct 2026 (RM-08, RM-09): every keyframe is declared in the kernel; a component may apply one and may not declare one. lv is struck until a surface draws level bars, and the three overlay entrances joined.

The ambient tempo rule. Motion measured in seconds means "this is live". Motion measured in milliseconds means "you did that". The two budgets never mix: retiming the sweep to the spring would make it mean "this just happened", and stretching a drawer to 17s would make an overlay claim to be an ongoing process. 🛑 Nothing animates that carries information a still frame does not — which is what makes the reduced-motion guard a complete answer rather than a degradation.

Reduced motion, and the four things that hold still

🛑 Under prefers-reduced-motion the spring still runs. It is not switched off and nothing is swapped for a crossfade — the distance is clamped to a few pixels and the spring settles wherever that lands it. A state change still reads as a change rather than a jump, and nobody gets a second, worse animation written for them.

The reason for clamping distance rather than cutting duration is that travel is what causes the problem. A long transition that moves nothing is comfortable; a short one that throws an element across the viewport is not.

What may never move, and why each one is on the list.
Never movesWhy
Anything carrying a number that mattersA figure in motion is a figure being read wrong. A count, an amount, a confidence or a spend appears in place and changes in place \u2014 and if it must change while somebody is looking at it, it changes without travelling.
Rows entering or leaving the registerThe register is the work. Rows that slide in on a filter make the list feel like a feed, and a reader loses their place in something they were counting.
A refusal appearingA refusal is a fact, not an event. Animating it in makes it look like something that happened rather than something that was always true, and it delays the sentence a person is waiting to read.
The status barIts grammar never rearranges, which is the whole feature. Motion in a fixed grammar is a change in a thing whose value is that it does not change.
A skeleton resolving into contentThe skeleton promised the shape. If the real content fades or slides over it, the promise was not kept \u2014 it should be a straight swap into the same box.

Which leaves the four product animations and the one brand animation named above, and nothing else. Anything that wants to move and is not on that list is a finding, not a local exception.

The mascot

∴ — three dots in a triangle. It belongs with the wordmark and the badge, not with the glyphs, and that placement is the whole ruling: a glyph may not move and may not carry state, because it is part of the product's language and it is counted. The mascot may move, because it reports nothing. It sits outside the seven glyphs and outside the six indicators for the same reason the logo does.

The two lines it may not cross

One brand animation, declared in the inventory and scoped to one selector. mascot-cascade may run on .mascot and nowhere else; a product surface reaching for it is a finding, exactly as a fifth product animation would be. Under prefers-reduced-motion the mascot goes still and stays a complete mark — unlike the connection light, it holds nothing back, because its resting state was never carrying anything.

Why there is no set

An icon vocabulary is a second semantic channel, and it would compete with the grammar for the same job — a mail glyph beside "waiting on the bank feed …" does not add information, it splits it. An icon set also grows by resemblance rather than by decision: the next icon arrives because a shape existed, which is precisely the pressure the six-indicator count exists to hold. And under R1 an icon cannot carry state anyway, so every icon the system could add would be decoration on a word that is already doing the work. The one mark this system does spend on a figure is its own: ∴ still, documented with the mark rather than here, and carrying the same line — the moment it is the only thing reporting something, it has become an indicator.

13 · Route-level states — live

Each state carries a scope and a time. A run that found nothing reads differently from a run that did not happen, and both read differently from a read that failed — three states one word apart, and the words are the whole difference.

Loading skeletons — and the three places they are the wrong answer

Missing from this system until now, which is why every slow surface in the product either says nothing or says …. Use shadcn/ui · Skeleton rather than hand-rolling one: it is a div with a pulse and the pulse is the part people get wrong.

A skeleton is a promise about shape. Draw one only where the shape that arrives is the shape you drew — a register row is 6 columns whatever the run found, a figure block is four figures, a case thread is message-shaped. Draw one where the shape is a guess and the page rearranges under the reader, which is worse than a wait.

What moved, and what did not

Three destinations, three tabs, and never a fourth — the same rule the desktop bar keeps, which is why the phone shell needs no overflow affordance. A tab bar that can hold exactly the destinations that exist is the strongest argument the three-destination rule has.

Six of the nine chrome controls are not dropped, they are relocated. The entity switcher, notifications, Setup and sign-out go behind the avatar; search stays because it is the route to everything else; the connection light, the token meter and the agent state move into the status sheet, because all three are reports rather than controls.

The status bar becomes one tappable row. Its grammar is "one grammar on every protected surface", so it may not be truncated and may not be dropped — it opens the full keys / provenance / audit trail as a sheet. The row shows the provenance slot because that is the half a person checks without being asked.

⚠️ The tabs are 44px tall even though the floor is 24. 2.5.8 is a conformance minimum, not a design target, and a primary destination reached with a thumb at the bottom edge of a phone is the one control in the system that genuinely wants the larger touch size.

The register triages rather than scrolls. Below 720px it drops to the three columns a person triages on and folds value, score and whose-move into the row's second line. It is the one table that reflows instead of scrolling, because it is the one surface a person reads for hours.

What is not covered

  • OpenNo AAA position. The target is AA and the system does not claim otherwise. --ink and --agent-deep clear AAA incidentally; the rest of the ramp does not, and raising it would collapse the distinction between --muted, --refused and --recede, which carry three different meanings.
  • Open2.4.11 is asserted, not tested. Focus Not Obscured depends on scroll behaviour that cannot be verified from a specimen sheet — it needs the real register at the real length, on a real keyboard.
  • OpenNo screen-reader transcript. The ARIA contract per primitive is specified below, and what a given reader actually announces is not the same claim. Three readers on three platforms is the test, and it has not been run.
  • OpenNo reduced-transparency or forced-colors position. The system has almost no transparency to reduce — the scrim, the hatch and the luminous touch — but forced-colors mode would flatten the luminous touch and the hatch to nothing, and the not-built treatment would lose both its halves. That is a real gap and a decision rather than a value.

The theming contract

The kernel is the source of truth and the exports are generated from it, never hand-maintained. Three formats ship, and the reason there are three is that they answer three different questions:

FileFormatWhat it is for
tokens.tokens.jsonW3C Design Tokens (DTCG)The interchange format. What a design tool reads and what any future generator should consume. Canonical.

🛑 The Tailwind preset must never inline a hex. A preset that copies #00303f into its palette forks the kernel: the dark theme stops applying, and there are two places a colour is declared. Every entry points at the custom property, which is why tokens.css still has to be loaded.

Built on shadcn/ui · what we take, what we re-skin, and what we refuse

The position: take the behaviour, replace the look, and never fork the token layer. shadcn is not a dependency that ships a stylesheet — it is source you own, built on Radix primitives, styled through variables. That is the only shape of component library this kernel can use, because the kernel is the variables and it does not negotiate.

So the rule for anyone building a new surface is: reach for the shadcn component first, and hand-roll only where the table below says there is nothing to reach for. A hand-rolled select, dialog, popover or combobox is a focus trap, a roving tabindex and six ARIA attributes somebody has to get right twice — and this system already has one of those written by hand in each console.

No component may carry a literal value. Not a hex, not a pixel radius, not a duration. shadcn's own convention is already this — bg-background, text-foreground, border-input — so adopting it costs one mapping file and buys the enforcement.

Rules in force

Nineteen. They bind the kernel and everything drawn against it. A rule broken locally is a finding, never a local exception.

  1. R1No state may depend on hue. There is no red, no amber and no traffic-light green, and --agent is deep precisely so it cannot be read as "matched". Every state carries a word; colour only ever reinforces one.
  2. R2Radius is 0 everywhere except the connection light. The badge is square too — --badge-radius is the single revert point.
  3. R3No second line, and no second dot. A progress bar is a second line. An unread dot is a second dot. Both were built once and both were deliberately removed.
  4. R4No shadow, and no elevation scale. A surface separates by rule or by ground. The two scrims are the only exception, and they exist because a modal must dim what it blocks.
  5. R5Six indicators. A seventh is a design change. Not an addition, and not a thing one component may mint on its own.
  6. R6The agent hue means the machine measured something, and nothing else. Not matched, good, passed or safe. Not a hover state, not a citation, and not whose turn it is. "The machine is working" is carried by ink at mark scale and by .lit at surface scale.
  7. R7Not-built is mandatory, and it names what refused it. Hatch, word, and reason. A refused control drawn as an ordinary one becomes evidence of a shipped control. A hatch without a reason is half the treatment.
  8. R8Tooltips are allowed, including for a disabled reason — reachable by focus and by tap, never hover-only. The reason is still stated beside the control; the tooltip is additive. A disabled control carries aria-disabled, never the disabled attribute, so a keyboard user can still reach the reason. The open state has exactly one writer, and it is the script.
  9. R9Every figure a person compares against another figure is monospaced with tabular numerals. So columns align and a digit never changes width between two renders of the same number.
  10. R10Keyboard first, and the focus ring is never optional. The ring is an outline, declared once. A component's own box-shadow edge cannot replace a different property at any specificity or source order, so the erasability is not defended against — it is unavailable.
  11. R11prefers-reduced-motion stops the sweep and the halo. Both kernel animations are ambient — they mean "this is live" — and neither carries information a still frame does not. The halo settles at .3 rather than 0, so a down light keeps two differences from a live one instead of one.
  12. R12Refused is its own ink step. --refused for a control that was never usable and for the sentence naming what refused it; --recede for cleared work; --muted for supporting text. Never a dimmed opacity — opacity composites against whatever is behind it, so it fails contrast silently and by a different amount on every ground.
  13. R13Type is paired, not chosen. Every step arrives with its weight, tracking and leading already decided. The three small steps are fixed; only the three above body are fluid, and they clamp with a rem floor rather than a vw one. Text measures are in ch and never in px.
  14. R14Four product animations, one spring, and a fifth is a finding. Anything that moves runs the spring on the way in and a plain fade on the way out — a leaving element does not bounce. Anything that only changes opacity or colour runs the fade, because it has no distance to ease across. Seconds mean "this is live"; milliseconds mean "you did that", and the two budgets never mix. The mascot's brand animation is counted separately under R19 and may run on nothing else.
  15. R15There is no icon set. Six glyphs, and a seventh is a design change. All six are typographic. Four carry meaning and are counted as indicators; two are affordance and are always aria-hidden inside a control that already has an accessible name. A glyph that is the only thing naming a control is a defect, not an icon. The metered mark is not a glyph — it is the mascot's still form, ruled under R19.
  16. R16The accent means wayfinding, and it reinforces rather than carries. Where you are, and what Enter would run. Not a status, not a severity, not a hover, not a measurement. Remove the accent and wayfinding still works — that is the test for whether a colour is carrying something it should not.
  17. R17Every interactive target clears 24 × 24, and the whole interface works at 320px. 2.5.8 and 1.4.10. The floor is declared once as a rule so a new control cannot be born under it, and it is in rem so a reader who raises their font size gets a bigger target. None of 2.5.8's three exceptions is used anywhere.
  18. R18Two themes, and no component knows which one it is in. The dark theme is a recompute, not an inversion or a filter: every step keeps its name and its job and takes a measured value of its own. A component that branches on the theme is a component with two designs.
  19. R19The mascot is brand, and brand may move. ∴ belongs with the wordmark, not with the glyphs, and it has two registers. STILL — the character, at text size — is the metered mark: it trails a spend figure and follows words that say an act is billable, and it needs no unit word beside it. MOVING is identity scale only, and its energy follows what the product is doing: working, thinking, listening. The mark never moves in a dense context, and in neither register is it the only thing reporting anything.

Findings — five closed, three open

A value a component needed and the kernel did not carry is a finding, never a local invention. Five that were carried open are closed in this kernel; three cannot be closed from it.

  • ClosedThere was no ink step meaning refused. --recede means cleared work and --muted means supporting text; a control that was never usable is neither, and every file reached for --muted because it was the only step that stayed above AA on the ground a disabled control is drawn on — a contrast argument standing in for a semantic. Minted as --refused, #4a6166, 6.57:1 on --paper and 6.07:1 on --sheet, with the disabled control and the not-built reason as its first two call sites.
  • ClosedThere was no z-index scale. The kernel wrote a bare z-index:99 on the skip link and named no others, so the bundle carried 2, 3, 4, 5 and 30 on comparable layers and kept them consistent with that 99 by hand. Four layers — --z-popover 20 · --z-overlay 30 · --z-modal 40 · --z-skip 99. The order is the decision rather than the numbers; they are spaced by ten so a component can sit between two without reopening the scale.
  • ClosedThere was no page measure, no grid floor and no breakpoint. Every file was calc(var(--u)*295) wide by hand, and the same was true of every grid column minimum and of the 720px breakpoint. --measure · --col-min · --bp-narrow. ⚠️ --bp-narrow is declared as a constant, not a usable token — a custom property cannot be read inside @media, so every breakpoint is still written literally. It is declared so the number has one home and a second breakpoint has to argue with it.
  • ClosedThe not-built hatch was fainter than the faintest line in the system. Measured at alpha .055 the stripe composited to 1.11:1 on all four grounds — below --rule at 1.38:1 — so a refused control rendered as an ordinary one with only the corner word surviving, on every ground including the one the alpha was calibrated for. Raised to .18: 1.42:1 on both light grounds. The reported premise that it was --sheet-specific was exactly inverted: an alpha composite scales the ground rather than displacing it, which is what makes an alpha stripe ground-independent, and the solid stripe the finding implied would have introduced the defect it named.
  • ClosedThe not-built reason had no home. The hatch says a thing is not built; the sentence naming the decision that refused it is what makes the hatch actionable, and five files invented five names for it — .nbwhy · .why-nb · .nb-why · .nbnote · .cap. One name, .nbwhy, beside .nb, in the refused step.
  • OpenThe state label has no agreed markup.
