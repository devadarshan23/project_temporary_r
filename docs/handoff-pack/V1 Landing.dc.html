The cost

None of this is abstract. It is a person opening a spreadsheet.

Every exception in the pile is somebody pulling a statement, calling a supplier, finding the goods receipt that was reversed, and writing down what they concluded so the next person does not have to work it out again. The work is not hard. There is simply too much of it, and it all lands in the same five days.

  • ~Days, not minutes. The close waits on exceptions, and exceptions wait on whoever has the context. The tilde is deliberate — that is an estimate, and the product marks every number it is guessing at.
  • ›The judgement is the valuable part, and it is the part that gets rushed, because the reading ate the time.
  • …And some of it is not yours to finish. A charge nobody can account for is blocked on a bank, a supplier or another team — work that is real, moving, and moving somewhere else.
How it reads

It reads the document before it matches it.

Any layout, any language. Fields are lifted with a confidence score, and anything below the floor is parked for a person to confirm rather than guessed at. That floor is set per document pack by our delivery team, not left to a default — a pack with no floor refuses to run.

The platform

One path, six stages — and a person owns the last one.

Everything in the product serves one of these or governs it. The agent runs every stage; naming it says nothing. A person in the loop is the exception, so that is what gets named.

1 · ArriveIntakeUpload, S3 or SFTP, email, or a connector into your source systems.
2 · ReadExtractionTyped values with a confidence each. Below the floor is parked, not guessed.
3 · PairMatchingDefinitions and rules produce match groups across systems.
4 · JudgeExceptionsSeverity, an explanation, and the candidates it ruled out.
5 · ActResolutionYour SOPs, as proposals. Above an authority limit it stops and waits.
6 · ShowReportingA register that states its scope and its denominator, and exports on a schedule.

Ask is a capability before it is a destination. Any question about the period, in words — no SQL, no export request, no ticket — answered from the source systems with a confidence and the cost of the answer. It opens over the register rather than taking you away from it.

Where it runs

Two deployments, and the harder one is the one enterprises ask for.

Your documents are the most sensitive thing in the close. So the question of whose account they sit in is not a deployment detail, it is the first thing a security review asks.

Hosted

We run it. Fastest to a first reconciliation, and the default for a partner-sold tenant.

Your data sits in our account under your retention and residency settings, and the audit trail is yours.

Your own AWS account · BYOA

The product deploys into your account. Your documents, your KMS keys, your Bedrock quota, your bill — and your connector credentials in your own Secrets Manager, which we never see.

The application is served from our CloudFront and your browser calls your own API directly, so tenant data never transits a 1CloudHub account. The code comes from us and executes against your data in your user's browser — stated plainly here because a security review will ask about exactly that.

  • ›Half of onboarding is yours. The key exchange, the model pre-flight, identity federation and the connector credentials all happen inside your account, by your people. We track a state per step so "why are we not live yet" always has an answer.
  • ›Your identity provider, your roles. OIDC or SAML, at your own host. There is no ReconcileAI password.
  • …Escalating hosted to your own account moves everything — documents, runs, resolutions, approvals and the audit trail. A partial migration is a lost history wearing a better word.

What it does not do

The refusals are the product, as much as the features are.

A reconciliation engine that quietly decides things is worth less than one that stops. These are deliberate, and they are on the record.

  • ✕It does not post above an authority limit. The agent proposes; a named person approves, with a required reason. The reason is what an SOP amendment gets drafted from.
  • ✕It does not guess a field below the confidence floor. It parks it and asks.
  • ✕No colour carries a state. Every status is a word. Nothing in the interface depends on your being able to tell green from amber.
  • ✕It does not answer questions it cannot evidence. An answer arrives with a confidence, and a confidence below the floor arrives as a question.
  • ✕There is no self-serve sign-up. A tenant carries a deployment mode, a residency, a retention policy and a support-access mode — four decisions with no safe defaults, so a person makes them with you.
