We never confirm whether an address belongs to a customer. A domain we do not recognise gets the same reply as one we do — because a reply that differed would tell anyone whether a company is a customer, and under bring-your-own-account a host name can carry their name.

Two ways an address resolves, and only one of them is ours to maintain. If your organisation has registered its own directory with us, your domain routes to it and your own sign-in decides who you are — we verify the domain by DNS before it routes anywhere, because an unverified claim on a domain is a way to capture somebody else's logins. Everyone else resolves against a list we keep, which is the older path and the one with the cost above.

Two consequences worth stating. Your directory decides membership, not employment — so a guest account in a customer's directory is refused here and sent to its own sign-in, because otherwise a partner's engineer would arrive as that customer's own person. And a directory tells us who someone is now, not who they were then, so the organisation is stamped on every act at the moment it happens: an approval made in March stays attributed to who they were in March.
